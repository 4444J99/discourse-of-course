## Part A — Region-Selection Guide

The commissioning package's workflow opens with "select the discourse" and, until now, gave no criteria. This guide supplies them. Two entry points: by what the work offers, and by what the desk needs to know.

### A.1 Selection by the work (object-driven)

| If the work... | Commission first |
|---|---|
| Is formally dense or strange at the sentence level | Work pole, manifest mode (formalist preset) |
| Has a documented crux — letters, drafts, a known life event fused to a textual moment | Maker pole, manifest (biographical preset) — only if the crux is documented; without documents the commission fails Gate 2 by design |
| Sits inside a contested or poorly mapped moment | World: circumstance (historical preset) |
| Has its ideology as the public question about it | World: structure, axis = the work's own obsession (the axis the text itself keeps returning to, not the desk's favorite) |
| Has a split, evolving, or heated reception | Receiver pole (reader-response preset), or reception history at work granularity |
| Is self-conscious about its own language; makes claims that visibly strain against their wording | Medium pole, unstable mode (deconstruction preset) |
| Raises career-arc or canon-stakes questions | Granularity oeuvre or canon; posture testing |
| Has been reviewed to death in the usual cells | Empty-cell logic: consult Category System §3.7, find the unoccupied coordinate (participant vantage? an unexamined axis? express aim at scale?), and commission that |

### A.2 Selection by the question (aim-driven)

| The desk needs to know... | Commission |
|---|---|
| Is it well made? | Work/manifest, craft standard, evaluate-heavy |
| What does it mean? | Interpret-dominant; pole = wherever meaning plausibly lives for this work (A.1 tells you where) |
| Should our readers read it? | Review recipe, evaluate + guide dominant, standard named and operationalized |
| What does it reveal about its world? | World/structure, named axis |
| Why do people fight over it? | Receiver pole; reception history |
| Both race and gender? | A region with two axes — first-class commission, no waiver needed (region rule, package §1.2) |

### A.3 Selection cautions

1. **Match mode to material.** Unstable mode needs a self-conscious text — deconstructing workmanly, transparent prose yields little; latent mode needs a text with symptomatic behavior (repetitions, excesses, absences worth reading); manifest mode is always available and is the default.
2. **Two axes maximum per 2,200-word review.** A third axis starves all three sections. Pole pairs only when the work genuinely locates meaning in both (the Bonaparte case).
3. **Do not commission maker-pole on hope.** If the documents don't exist yet, the commission doesn't exist yet.
4. **Portfolios over monoliths.** For canonical works, commission across contrasting regions and sequence for contrast (package §3.4): the portfolio's claim — that the regions are complementary instruments — is the series thesis.

## Part B — Commercial Terms Schedule

The package governed quality and said nothing about money. This schedule is a fill-in template with defaults drawn from common freelance editorial practice; ranges are negotiable per writer and market. **This is a starting template, not legal advice — have counsel review before use in any jurisdiction.**

| Term | Default | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Fee | {PER_WORD_RATE} per word, or flat {FLAT_FEE} | Per-word remains standard for reviews; flat fees suit essays |
| Payment schedule | 50% on acceptance, 50% on publication; net-30 on invoices | Full fee on acceptance acceptable for established relationships |
| Rights | First serial rights; 90-day exclusivity from publication; non-exclusive digital archive thereafter | All other rights retained by the writer |
| Kill fee | 25–50% of fee, rights revert to writer | For commissioned work accepted in concept but not run |
| Revisions | Two rounds (R1, R2) included in fee; R3 triggers the kill-fee decision per package §3.2 | The three-round house rule is contractual, not just editorial |
| Late delivery | Grace period of {X_DAYS}; thereafter the desk may cancel at 50% | Extensions granted once, in writing |
| AI use | Disclosure required at pitch; writer remains liable for every claim, quotation, and citation (package §1.3) | Undisclosed AI use treated as misrepresentation |
| Plagiarism / fabrication | No fee; termination; clawback of fees paid | Gate 2 automatic rejection escalates to contract, not just QA |
| Confidentiality | Manuscript not shared, quoted, or summarized before publication | Extends to commissioning correspondence |
| Termination | Either party, per kill-fee terms | The writer's file note (package §3.2) governs re-commissioning |

**Contracting rule:** the commission's coordinate block is appended to the schedule as an exhibit. The region is not just an editorial instruction — it is the deliverable's specification of goods.

## Part C — Gate-3 Editor Training and Calibration

Gate 3 (region fidelity) requires an editor who can recognize, on the page, where a piece's attention actually lives. This section trains that recognition.

### C.1 Pole recognition cues

| Pole | What the prose looks like |
|---|---|
| **Work** | Sentences quote the text and describe what the quotation *does* ("the image degrades," "the sentence enacts"); evidence is internal; no dates except publication; characters discussed as functions of the design |
| **Maker** | Letters, diaries, drafts, composition dates; graded-inference vocabulary ("documented," "probable," "speculative"); the life doing argumentative work |
| **Medium** | Language treated as a system: etymology, signifiers, a word's behavior outrunning the sentence's claim; the object is the wording itself as behavior, not as design |
| **World: circumstance** | Coeval documents, period detail, reception events, "at the time"; context doing argumentative work, not decoration |
| **World: structure** | Power vocabulary; a named axis or the general reading; symptomatic moves ("what the text cannot say," "despite itself") |
| **Receiver** | Cue → response chains; temporal reading ("by the time the reader reaches"); reading communities, horizons of expectation |

### C.2 Mode recognition cues

| Mode | Vocabulary signature |
|---|---|
| **Manifest** | Inventory of what is there: devices, structures, patterns — "the text does" |
| **Latent** | Symptom, repression, absence, return, "what it cannot stop doing," "more or other than it says" |
| **Unstable** | Self-undermining, reversal, aporia, undecidability — "the text convicts itself," "cannot decide" |

### C.3 Drift symptoms (the masked-reader's field guide)

| Commissioned region | Drift smells like |
|---|---|
| Work / manifest | A sentence about the author's life or the book's reception doing argumentative work |
| World: structure, axis class | Unnamed gender analysis appearing mid-essay and carrying weight |
| Receiver pole | Psychoanalysis of readers; "what the author really feared" |
| Maker / manifest | Psychology beyond the documents; the biographical fallacy |
| Medium / unstable | The critic imposing a reversal without quoted textual evidence — the stress test's Case 8 constraint, visible on the page as assertion without quotation |

### C.4 Calibration protocol

1. **Blind-classify** the fifteen pieces in the validation corpus (the answer key is that document's table).
2. Score agreement on **pole and mode** — the two slots Gate 3 exists to police.
3. **80% agreement clears the editor for Gate-3 duty.** Below that: re-read Category System Parts 1–2, re-test on five fresh pieces.
4. **Two standing rules from the validation findings:** blocks attach to texts, not authors (classify the piece in hand); classification is not verification (a piece can be perfectly in-region and factually wrong — that is Gate 2's failure, not Gate 3's).
5. **Recalibrate quarterly**, and after any Gate-3 dispute, with the disputed piece added to the corpus.
