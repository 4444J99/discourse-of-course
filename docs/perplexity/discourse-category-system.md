# The Category System
## Discourse About Art, Built From First Principles

**Document type:** Foundational taxonomy — replaces the parallel taxonomies (personas, forms, discourses, aesthetic families) with one derived system
**Version:** 1.1 (stress-tested; see the companion stress-test log)
**Date:** November 2026
**Relationship to prior artifacts:** Subsumes the twelve-plus-six persona gallery, the form list, the eight critical discourses, the aesthetic-family matrix, the vantage dimension, and the commissioning package's template set. All of them are derived views of this system (Part 3).

---

## Part 1 — The Floor

### 1.1 The founding fact

There is a work, and there is everything said about the work that is not the work. The second thing is discourse about art. This is the top of the hierarchy — the "drink" level — and it is a natural class: it exists whether or not anyone categorizes it.

Everything below this level is a category. Categories are cuts, and cuts can be made anywhere, which is why classification feels endlessly extensible. The question this system answers is: which cuts are load-bearing, and which are just shelf arrangement?

### 1.2 The removal test

A claimed category is a **primitive** only if removing it eliminates the object itself. If you can delete the category and discourse about art still exists intact, the category is a convenience, not a foundation — it belongs at the shelf level, not the floor.

This test is what separates the two problems that usually get conflated: *endless subdivision* (horizontal growth — always possible, never bottoms out) and *reduction* (asking what categories are made of — bottoms out exactly when the removal test stops passing). Run the test on every claim below.

### 1.3 The circuit

A literary work cannot exist without five elements. Delete any one and the object vanishes:

| Element | Definition | Removal test |
|---|---|---|
| **Maker** | The person and conditions of making | A work made by no one is not a work |
| **Work** | The artifact itself | No artifact, no object of discourse |
| **Medium** | The language or material it is made of | A work made of nothing cannot be made |
| **World** | The context and structures it is made in | Making outside any world is not making |
| **Receiver** | The audience it is made for | A work for no one is not communication — it is a private symptom |

These five are the only places meaning can be located, and therefore the only possible primary objects of critical attention.

The five elements are **roles in a circuit, not persons**. One person can occupy several — the poet-performer, the player who completes a game by playing it, the novelist reviewing their own book — and a role can be occupied by an unknown (the anonymous folk song) or by a process (the AI-generated work, whose conditions of making — model, corpus, prompt — are the maker pole's content). Attention, to be legitimate, must land on the role: the maker *as maker*, the receiver *as receiver*.

This floor is not an invention. M. H. Abrams derived four of the five poles independently in *The Mirror and the Lamp* (1953) — universe, audience, author, work — as the complete set of orientations available to criticism. Roman Jakobson's communication model (1960) supplies all five plus contact, with a language function pegged to each. Two traditions, sixty years apart, hitting the same floor from different directions is the strongest evidence that the floor is real.

### 1.4 The acts

Discourse is speech, and speech can only do a small number of things to its listener. At the working level, five:

1. **Describe** — say what it is
2. **Interpret** — say what it means
3. **Evaluate** — say what it is worth
4. **Guide** — say what to do (read it, skip it, make it differently)
5. **Express** — say how it struck you

At the philosophical floor these reduce to three (Searle's assertives, directives, expressives: describe/interpret/evaluate are all assertions, guide is direction, expression is expression). The five are the working level — the basic level people actually think in, in the sense that "soda" is thought in more readily than "cola" or "beverage."

Two definitions the working level needs: the acts are **functional, not intentional** — defined by what the discourse does to its receiver, not by what its author meant (the recommendation algorithm performs a guide act without a guiding mind) — and **medium-independent**: they can be performed without sentences. A ranking guides; a cover version interprets; a parody evaluates. Making can be discourse.

### 1.5 The modes

Meaning, wherever it is located, behaves in one of three ways:

| Mode | Claim about meaning | Founding discourse |
|---|---|---|
| **Manifest** | Meaning is constituted by what is present and available in the work — not shallow, but made of what is there | Formalist |
| **Latent** | Meaning lies beneath the manifest; the work means more or other than it says | Psychoanalytic, Marxist |
| **Unstable** | Meaning undoes itself; the work's own language subverts its claims | Deconstruction |

One related question — is meaning *found* or *made*? — needs no separate slot: it is implied by the pole. If meaning lives in the work or the medium, it is found; if it lives in the receiver, it is made in the encounter.

### 1.6 The legitimacy test

The circuit does double duty: it is the floor of the category system and the admission rule for membership. **Legitimate discourse directs its attention at the circuit** — at the work, its making, or its audience's experience of it.

Payoff redirection names the motive but not the line — every working reviewer is paid and builds reputation. The operational test is **substitutability**: if the object could be swapped for another without the discourse losing its function, the attention is counterfeit. Rage bait works with any target; sycophancy with any patron; trend-following with any trend. Legitimate discourse is non-substitutable — it could only have been written about this work. Combined with the roles clause (the maker as maker, not as celebrity), the test excludes gossip and fandom-mockery as well: they attend to the occupant of a circuit role, not the role.

A failed piece is not a new category waiting to be classified. It is a failed member of an existing one. This is why the counterfeits would not place in the persona matrix: the matrix's admission requirement was the circuit, stated informally. This system states it formally.

## Part 2 — The Coordinates

Every act of discourse about art is a point in a seven-slot coordinate space. Five slots are closed (fixed value sets, from the floor); three are open (extensible, by design). The closed slots carry the reduction; the open slots quarantine the endlessness.

| Slot | Values | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Object** | pole (5) × world-reading (2) × axis (open) × granularity (4) × cardinality (2) | Closed poles, open axis |
| **Mode** | manifest / latent / unstable | Closed |
| **Aim** | describe / interpret / evaluate / guide / express, in proportion | Closed |
| **Standard** | the value criterion (required when evaluate is present) | Slowly growing menu |
| **Vantage** | participant / reconstructive / encounter | Closed |
| **Posture** | affirming / testing / suspending | Closed |
| **Format** | the recipe (see 2.8) | Open |

### 2.1 Object

The object slot answers *what the discourse is about* — which is really two questions: where in the circuit attention is directed, and at what scale.

**Pole (closed, 5):** maker / work / medium / world / receiver. This is the primary cut. Every critical discourse in history is a commitment to one pole as the site of meaning.

**World-reading (only when pole = world):** the world pole has two readings, and the distinction matters —
- **Circumstance:** what happened — events, period, conditions of production and reception. Plain historical attention.
- **Structure:** whose interests — the power relations the work participates in. Structural attention, which names an axis.

**Axis (open, only under structure):** class, gender, race, colonial relation, species, region, religion, disability, sexuality, and onward — or **general** (power as such: the New Historicist reading, structural attention without a single named axis). This is the one slot in the system that subdivides forever, because power has infinitely many axes. Marxist, feminist, postcolonial, ecocritical, and critical-race criticism are not five poles — they are one pole (world: structure) under five axes. The endlessness of the shelf is real, but it is quarantined: it lives inside a single slot of a single pole. The floor stays five.

**Granularity (closed, 4):** work / oeuvre / canon / form. Judging a work, a career, a canon, and an art form are different acts at different scales, and a system that only has the work scale cannot see the others.

**Cardinality (closed, 2):** singular / comparative. One work, or many. Comparison is not a persona or a discourse — it is the object's cardinality.

**Layer (optional, for performed and versioned forms):** text / production / performance (extensible to edition, cut, draft). A production review and a text analysis of the same play are different coordinates at the same pole; the layer specifies which identity of the work is the object.

### 2.2 Mode

Manifest / latent / unstable, as defined at the floor (1.5). Mode is multi-select with a primary — a discourse may read latently and track instability at once (e.g., Žižek). The mode and pole together generate every critical discourse there is or can be (Part 3.1).

### 2.3 Aim

The five acts, selected and weighted. Most real discourse combines them: a review is describe (15%) + interpret (40%) + evaluate (30%) + guide (15%) by proportion, roughly. The proportions are the recipe's skeleton.

### 2.4 Standard

Whenever evaluate is **explicitly** present — a verdict section, a ranking, a grade — the standard must be named and **operationalized** (state what would count as meeting it: "the work must make its social world legible in its form," not "significant"). Implicit evaluation (emphasis, selection, inclusion) is handled by aim proportions, not by this slot. The criterion of worth: The menu is populated by value theories — craft (value is achieved form), expression (value is felt truth), effect (value is response produced), mimesis (value is truth to the world), originality, significance — and in this project by the aesthetic families already defined in the persona-by-aesthetic-family matrix, which occupy exactly this slot. An evaluation without a named standard is not a category error; it is an incomplete coordinate, and it is the most common incompleteness in published criticism.

### 2.5 Vantage

Where the speaker stands relative to the making:

- **Participant** — inside the making: manifestos, self-analysis, criticism enacted through making (the cover version, the parody, the homage)
- **Reconstructive** — outside, facing the making: biographical attention, drafts and process, peer feedback addressed to a maker's choices
- **Encounter** — outside, facing the work: nearly all published criticism

These three values are **anchor points on a continuum**, not partitions — the biographer who interviews their subject for twenty years stands between reconstructive and participant; classification assigns the nearest anchor, and mixed cases state their mixture. The dimension was the matrix's thirteenth. It is retained here because it passes the removal test from the speaker's side: discourse is itself an act, and an act happens from somewhere.

### 2.6 Posture

The epistemic attitude of the speaker toward received views:

- **Affirming** — extends and applies the received understanding
- **Testing** — checks claims, assumptions, and reputations against evidence, including its own
- **Suspending** — holds possibilities open, preserves uncertainty where warranted

Like vantage, these are anchor points on a continuum: a review can affirm a reputation while testing its individual claims; mixed cases state their mixture. Skeptic and Explorer, which resisted placement as personas, are simply this slot's non-default values. So is the rehabilitator: a reassessment is testing posture at canon granularity — it presupposes an earlier judgment and exists only in sequence.

### 2.7 Format (derived)

Formats are not primitives; they are **recipes** — fixed proportions of aims, aimed at an audience, at a length, in a delivery medium, in a structure. The recipe's ingredients are all closed-set values; the recipes themselves are open, because containers get invented. Review, essay, commentary, peer feedback, thread, video essay, rating, ranking, syllabus, blurb — each is a recipe, and new ones (the algorithmic ranking included: guide aim, work-scale object, encounter vantage, implicit standard, no sentences) enter the system without a single new primitive.

### 2.8 What the closed/open split buys

The system's guarantee: **anything that will ever count as discourse about art is a value assignment to these seven slots.** New discourses (the 2035 arrival) enter as new values in the axis or format slots — or as a new combination of closed values — and are classified on arrival. The endless shelf is real and safely contained; the floor is finite and complete.

## Part 3 — Derivations

Everything this project has already built is a view of the coordinate space. Nothing is discarded; everything is relocated.

### 3.1 The eight critical discourses as coordinates

| Discourse | Pole | Mode | Axis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formalist | work | manifest | — |
| Biographical | maker | manifest | — |
| Historical | world: circumstance | manifest | — |
| Psychoanalytic | work (or any) | latent | — |
| Reader-response | receiver | manifest | — |
| Marxist | world: structure | latent | class |
| Feminist | world: structure | manifest or latent | gender |
| Deconstruction | medium | unstable | — |

The "eight primary discourses" of the commissioning package are eight fixed points in this space — one flavor each of the soda. Note what the table exposes: feminist criticism's two strands (Millett's manifest reading of explicit content, Gilbert and Gubar's latent reading of the anxiety of authorship) are two coordinates, not one discourse — the strand problem dissolves into precision. And the discourses the textbook shelf left off are not missing rows; they are the same table continued: postcolonial criticism is world: structure under a colonial axis, ecocriticism is world: structure under a species axis, myth criticism (Frye) is world: circumstance at canon granularity with a comparative cardinality, structuralism is the medium pole under a manifest mode.

### 3.2 Hybrids are regions, not violations

A feminist-postcolonial reading is world: structure with two values in the axis slot. The commissioning package's single-lens rule treated this as method drift — a structural failure. In the coordinate system it is simply a region: two axes, one pole, one mode. The rule that replaces the single-lens rule is the **coordinate-region rule**: a commission specifies a region, and drift means leaving the region. Hybrid commissions are legitimate; unannounced drift is not. The difference between the two is now mechanical to check.

### 3.3 The forms as recipes

| Form | Aims (proportion) | Audience | Vantage | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Review | describe, interpret, evaluate, guide | General reader | Encounter | Work scale; the verdict is the evaluate section given directive force |
| Commentary | interpret, express | Public, topical | Encounter | Shorter; anchored to an occasion |
| Contextual essay | describe, interpret | Curious reader | Reconstructive or encounter | World: circumstance pole |
| Peer feedback | describe, evaluate, guide | The maker | Reconstructive | The only form whose addressee is on the circuit |
| Sustained criticism | interpret, evaluate | Scholarly | Encounter | Canon granularity permitted |
| Vernacular response | express, guide | Community | Encounter | The "I cried" register — fully classifiable: receiver pole, express aim |

The eight "discourse forms" from the project's first session (criticism, review, peer feedback, description, analysis, interpretation, commentary, contextual essay) reduce to these recipes — and reduce further to the five acts. Description and analysis are the describe act at two depths; interpretation is the interpret act; the rest are recipes.

### 3.4 The personas as clusters

The twelve cleaned orientations (and the six blind-spot personas) map onto the coordinates:

| Persona | Coordinate reading |
|---|---|
| Observer | work pole, describe aim, testing posture |
| Analyst | work pole, describe + interpret, encounter |
| Interpreter | work pole, interpret |
| Psychologist | latent mode, maker or work pole |
| Sociologist | world: structure, interpret (axis unspecified) |
| Historian | world: circumstance, interpret |
| Comparatist | cardinality: comparative |
| Empath | receiver pole, interpret + express |
| Collaborator | maker pole, guide aim, reconstructive vantage |
| Judge | evaluate aim with a named standard |
| Skeptic | posture: testing |
| Explorer | posture: suspending |
| Maker (13) | vantage: participant |
| The blind-spot personas (13–18) | the non-default values of vantage, posture, and granularity |

Personas are not a parallel taxonomy. They are **shorthand for common clusters** — some are single-slot markers (Skeptic, Explorer, Maker), some are multi-slot profiles. This is why the same person could appear under several personas in the example work: personas are regions, and regions overlap. The decoupling principle the project established — persona describes orientation, form assigned independently — is now structural: the persona slots (pole, mode, posture, vantage) and the form slot (recipe) are different coordinates, assignable independently by construction.

### 3.5 Aesthetic families as the Standard slot

The aesthetic families from the matrix (the accounts of where artistic value comes from) are the menu for the Standard slot. They apply exactly when evaluate is present. A persona without an aesthetic was an incomplete profile; here, an evaluation without a standard is an incomplete coordinate. Same insight, now enforced by the system rather than remembered by the editor.

### 3.6 The counterfeits as test failures

Rage bait, sycophancy, trend-following fail the legitimacy test (1.6): payoff redirected to the speaker, attention off the circuit. They have no coordinates because they have no object. The system does not need a counterfeit row — the test handles them at admission.

### 3.7 The formerly missing populations as coordinates

The matrix audit's six absent populations are, in this system, simply **addressable cells**:

| Gap | Coordinate |
|---|---|
| The making side (participant-as-criticism) | vantage: participant — now reachable |
| The unprofessional (vernacular criticism) | receiver pole, express aim, informal recipes |
| The selectors (curators, jurors, algorithms) | guide aim without sentences; format: ranking |
| The framework-builders (theorists) | canon granularity, medium or world pole |
| Time (rehabilitator, obituarist, archivist) | posture: testing at canon/oeuvre granularity; reception as object |
| Scale (career, canon, art form) | granularity: oeuvre / canon / form |

The audit's conclusion — "the matrix is a spectator's system: professional, synchronic, small-scale" — was the diagnosis of a shelf that only stocked one cell of each dimension. The coordinate space makes the empty cells visible, which makes them commissionable. That is the practical difference between a shelf and a space.

## Part 4 — The Rules

### 4.1 The naming rule

Things are named by their coordinates, in plain language. No brand names, no nicknames, no cutesy labels. "A latent-mode reading of the work pole" — not "The Depth Analyst." A coordinate name is longer but self-explanatory, stable across regroupings, and cannot be mistaken for a person. Where a short handle is needed for workflow (a file name, a commission code), the handle is an abbreviation of the coordinates, never a persona.

### 4.2 The classification procedure

To place any piece of discourse about art — existing, historical, or not yet invented:

1. **Run the legitimacy test.** Does the attention land on the circuit (the work, its making, or its audience's experience)? If no: counterfeit; stop.
2. **Assign the pole.** Where does the piece locate meaning? (If it never states or implies a location, it is incomplete, not unclassifiable.)
3. **If pole = world: structure, assign the axis.** One value, several, or unspecified.
4. **Assign granularity and cardinality.** What scale, how many works.
5. **Assign the mode(s), primary first.** Manifest, latent, or unstable — how does the piece treat meaning?
6. **Inventory the aims, in proportion.** What does it do, and in what ratio?
7. **If evaluate is present, extract the standard.** Unnamed means incomplete.
8. **Assign vantage and posture.** Where does the speaker stand; what is the attitude to received views?
9. **Identify the recipe.** Which format, or which new mixture of aims/audience/length/delivery?

The output is a seven-line coordinate block. Two pieces with the same block are the same kind of discourse regardless of era, medium, or prestige — a Letterboxd review and a nineteenth-century periodical notice with matching blocks are the same recipe.

### 4.3 The hybrid rule

A commission may specify any region of the space: one pole or two, one axis or three, one mode. The region is the contract. **Drift = leaving the region.** This replaces the single-lens rule with something both more permissive (hybrids are first-class) and more checkable (the region is a written coordinate block, and the QA reader compares the piece against it, not against a shelf of eight).

### 4.4 The generation rule

Templates are generated, not authored. The rule:

- **Sections come from the aims.** Each selected aim contributes its section: describe → an orientation section; interpret → the analysis sections; evaluate → a verdict section; guide → a recommendation section; express → a response section. Word budgets follow the aim proportions.
- **Content comes from the pole and mode.** The interpret sections are filled by the pole's characteristic evidence (maker: documents; work: devices; medium: language; world: coeval sources and structures; receiver: cues and response) and read in the mode's characteristic way (manifest: what is there; latent: what is underneath; unstable: what undoes itself).
- **The verdict section states the standard.** If evaluate is present, the criterion is named before the judgment is made.

This single rule generates all eight of the commissioning package's templates — and every template the package could not hold. The eight canned templates survive as presets: eight coordinate blocks with their generated sections already budgeted. They are conveniences, not the system.

### 4.5 The recursion rule

Every act of discourse is itself a made object with its own circuit, and every made object can be read as discourse. Therefore: **coordinates always apply to the immediate object.** A review of a biography is encounter-vantage discourse about a work (the biography as artifact). A critique of deconstruction is discourse about a family of discourse-works at canon granularity. An adaptation is a work (as an object of criticism) and an act of participant-vantage interpretation (as discourse) — two readings answering two questions, both well-formed. Second-order discourse is first-order discourse about a discourse-work. No infinite regress in practice, because each level classifies against its immediate object only — including this system classifying the document that classifies it.

## Part 5 — Commissioning On The New System

### 5.1 The commission spec

A commission is a coordinate block plus a recipe plus constraints. Replaces the welded persona+discourse+template prompt:

```
COMMISSION: Critical review of {WORK_TITLE} by {AUTHOR} ({PUBLICATION_YEAR})

OBJECT:
  pole: world (structure)
  axis: race, gender
  granularity: work
  cardinality: singular
MODE: latent (primary), unstable (secondary)
AIMS: describe 15% / interpret 55% / evaluate 30%
STANDARD: significance (what the work reveals and withholds about its world)
VANTAGE: encounter
POSTURE: testing
FORMAT: review recipe — educated general reader, {REVIEW_WORD_COUNT} words,
  {DELIVERY}, verdict section carries recommendation force

CONSTRAINTS:
  - Stay inside the region: pole world (structure, axes race and gender),
    modes latent (primary) and unstable (secondary). Drift outside the
    region is a structural failure.
  - Every factual claim sourced; every textual claim quoted.
  - Standard named in the verdict before judgment is rendered.
  - Plot summary ≤10% of body.

DUE: {DUE_DATE}
```

### 5.2 What the generation rule produces from that spec

| Section (from aim) | Budget (from proportion) | Content (from pole and mode) |
|---|---|---|
| Orientation | ~330 w | The work, the region declared, the thesis |
| Structure under axis: race | ~600 w | What the work says and cannot say about race — the symptomatic reading |
| Structure under axis: gender | ~600 w | The same machinery under the second axis |
| Verdict | ~440 w | Judgment against the named standard, with recommendation force |
| Works Cited | — | Per citation style |

A feminist-postcolonial review of, say, *Wide Sargasso Sea* — impossible to commission cleanly under the single-lens rule — is a routine output of this spec. No rule was relaxed to permit it; the rule was replaced by a better one.

### 5.3 QA redefined

The four intake gates survive, with one changed and one strengthened:

- **Gate 1 (structure)** — unchanged: sections match the generated template.
- **Gate 2 (citation)** — unchanged.
- **Gate 3 (region fidelity)** — replaces discourse fidelity: the masked-reader question becomes "does this piece stay inside the coordinate block?" Drift is defined by the commission's own region, not by a fixed shelf.
- **Gate 4 (rubric)** — unchanged, with the addition: if evaluate is present, a named standard is a scoring item.

### 5.4 What this supersedes

| Old artifact | Status in the new system |
|---|---|
| Eight discourse templates | Presets: eight coordinate blocks with pre-generated sections |
| Persona welds (persona + discourse + template) | Dissolved: pole, vantage, posture, and recipe are independently assignable slots |
| Single-lens rule | Replaced by the coordinate-region rule (4.3) |
| The persona gallery (12 + 6) | Retained as shorthand for common clusters; derived, not foundational |
| The aesthetic families | Retained as the Standard slot menu |
| The vantage dimension | Retained as a coordinate slot, unchanged |
| The form list | Retained as recipes; reduced to the five acts |
| The counterfeits | Handled by the legitimacy test, not by enumeration |
| The commissioning package's QA and revision protocol | Retained; Gate 3 redefined, standard-check added |

The commissioning package is not invalidated — it becomes the first preset library of the new system, with its conflicts (coupling, the single-lens rule, brand-name personas) resolved by derivation.

## Closing: What Changed

Three moves, in order:

1. **The floor was found by the removal test**, not by surveying the shelf: five circuit elements, five acts reducing to three, three modes. Anything below this is a cut, not a foundation.
2. **The seven coordinate slots were derived from the floor**: closed where the floor is closed (pole, mode, aims, vantage, posture), open only where growth is real (axis, format, standard). The endlessness of classification is not denied — it is quarantined in two slots.
3. **Everything the project built was relocated, not replaced**: discourses are points, hybrids are regions, personas are clusters, forms are recipes, aesthetic families are standards, counterfeits are test failures, and the populations the audits found missing are addressable cells.

The system's one promise: any act of discourse about art — past, present, or not yet invented — is a value assignment to these seven slots, and any commission is a region in that space. Everything else is shelf arrangement.
