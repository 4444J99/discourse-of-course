# Persona by aesthetic family matrix

This matrix crosses the twelve personas from the preceding document with eight aesthetic families: the persona names the **action** a critic performs in a particular work; the aesthetic family names the critic's **account of where artistic value comes from**. The two axes are independent, which is why the same person can appear in different cells.

Every person from the thirty-six examples appears once per persona, in the cell where their cited work belongs. Repeated names appear in multiple rows; some repeat in the same family, others in different families.

The matrix also carries a thirteenth dimension, **vantage**: where the critic stands relative to the making of the work. It is presented separately after the family matrix, because it cross-cuts both axes.

Two further sections extend the framework: counterfeit personas, in which the payoff is substituted for the account of value, and six new personas (13–18) converted from the matrix's blind spots, with their own placements in both matrices.

## The eight aesthetic families

Each family answers the same three questions differently: what is an artwork, where does value live, and what makes a work good.

| Family | Account of value |
| :-- | :-- |
| Formalist | Value lies in achieved form: structure, technique, verbal or cinematic precision. The artwork is an autonomous object of choices. |
| Depth-hermeneut | Value lies in latent meaning: symbols, archetypes, unconscious conflict. The artwork is a surface to be read through. |
| Sociologist of taste | Value judgments are social facts. The artwork is a site of power, class, and representation; "taste" itself is an object of study. |
| Historicist | Value is revealed through circumstances of creation and reception. The artwork is a document within a historical transaction. |
| Process / craft | Value is realized in making and collaboration. The artwork is an ongoing process refined through practice, editing, and dialogue. |
| Reader-centered | Value is located in audience experience and use: recognition, enchantment, nourishment, resistance. |
| Evaluative expressivist | Value lies in expressive achievement, judged by strong, contestable personal standards. The artwork is a feat of imagination. |
| Sensualist / pluralist | Value lies in sensuous immediacy and plurality of meaning; interpretation that forecloses either is suspect. |

## Reading rules

- The persona belongs to the **action demonstrated in the cited work**, not permanently to the person.
- The aesthetic family belongs to the **account of value that work assumes or argues for**.
- A cell entry therefore records a pairing of action and account — not a permanent classification of a person.

## What the filled and empty cells show

**Homogeneous rows.** Analyst, Interpreter, Psychologist, Sociologist, Historian, Empath, Collaborator, and Judge each occupy a single family. These personas select for a particular aesthetics: analyzing technique attracts formalists, interpreting symbols attracts depth-hermeneuts, considering audience experience attracts reader-centered critics, and so on. The alignment is strong enough that these rows nearly define the families.

**Heterogeneous rows.** Observer, Comparatist, Skeptic, and Explorer spread across two or three families. Testing assumptions and pursuing possibilities are compatible with almost any account of value, which is why the Skeptic row contains a sensualist (Sontag), a reader-centered critic (Felski), and an expressivist (Kael).

**Empty cells as findings.** No formalist appears as a Judge or Sociologist; critics who locate value in form rarely make social analysis or canon formation their evaluative basis. No depth-hermeneut appears as a Collaborator; decoding latent meaning and serving a writer's developing intention are different enterprises. No reader-centered critic appears as an Analyst or Observer. The sociologist-of-taste family appears only in the Sociologist row, and the reader-centered family only in the Empath, Skeptic, and Explorer rows — the personas that meet audiences rather than works. These absences are substantive, not accidents of sampling.

**The reflexive cell.** Bourdieu is the one entry whose aesthetic position denies the matrix's premise. A sociologist of taste holds that the "pure gaze" is itself a product of class and education, so his cell does not locate value so much as explain why anyone would locate it there. The matrix contains its own critic.

**Cross-overs that prove the axes are independent.**

- Barthes is an Observer with a sensualist aesthetics and an Explorer with a pluralist aesthetics: attention to detail, then attention to possibility, with the same underlying account of value.
- hooks is a Sociologist with a taste-sociology aesthetics and an Empath with a reader-centered aesthetics: analyzing structures of power, then distinguishing spectators' experiences.
- Kael occupies the evaluative-expressivist cell twice — as a Judge when assessing a film and as a Skeptic when testing auteur theory. Same family, different action.
- Felski and Sedgwick each appear twice in reader-centered cells across different personas, showing a stable aesthetics serving different critical moves.

## The thirteenth dimension: vantage

The Collaborator and Judge rows are mirror images: one locates value inside the making of the work, the other outside it. That observation is a dimension in its own right — the critic's **vantage**, or standing point relative to the making. It takes three positions:

- **Participant** — inside the making. Value is realized through practice, editing, and collaboration.
- **Reconstructive** — outside, facing the making. The work is explained by the choices, conditions, or mechanisms that produced it.
- **Encounter** — outside, facing the work. The critic attends to the work as met: its achieved form, its meaning, its effect, its judgment.

### What the vantage dimension adds

**It splits the depth-hermeneut family.** The Interpreter row is encounter — Panofsky, Frye, and Campbell read meaning in the finished work. The Psychologist row is reconstructive — Freud, Jones, and Bettelheim explain a work's power by hidden generative mechanisms. Same family, opposite directions of attention.

**It splits the formalist family.** Vendler and Wood encounter the achieved verbal object: they attend to what is on the page. Bordwell, Thompson, and Zhou reverse-engineer the choices that produce effects: they attend to how it was made to work.

**It splits the Sociologist row along production and reception.** Bourdieu reconstructs the social production of taste; hooks and Berger confront representation and spectatorship as they operate on audiences.

**Participants exist only in the Collaborator row.** No other persona works from inside the making. Criticism as practiced across these thirty-six examples is overwhelmingly an outside activity — thirty-three entries are reconstructive or encounter — and the lone inside row is the one populated by editors and writers rather than by critics.

**The Judge and Collaborator rows are mirror images on both dimensions.** All evaluative expressivist and all encounter, versus all process/craft and all participant.

**Vantage is the stable property; persona is the variable one.** Every person who appears more than once — Auerbach, Frye, hooks, Kael, Felski, Sedgwick, Barthes — keeps the same vantage across their appearances while changing personas. The action changes with the assignment; the standing point stays with the critic. Six of the seven repeaters also keep the same aesthetic family; hooks is the exception, moving from taste-sociology to reader-centered when the task becomes distinguishing spectators' experiences. She marks the boundary of the generalization: vantage never follows the task, family almost never does, and persona always does.

## Counterfeit personas

Three familiar figures of contemporary discourse fail the matrix's admission requirement: every legitimate cell directs attention at the work, its making, or its audience's experience, and every family locates value somewhere in that circuit. These three redirect the payoff away from the circuit and toward the actor. Each is a **forgery of a legitimate cell** — the same surface action, severed from the account of value that licenses it.

**The Rage Baiter** is a counterfeit Judge and Skeptic. It wears the Judge's force and the Skeptic's contrarianism, but the Judge persona is defined by standards that can be examined — the clause the Rage Baiter deletes. Its aesthetics is reader-centered, inverted: where the reader-centered family locates value in what the work does for the audience, the Rage Baiter locates value in what the audience does for the baiter. Same facing direction, opposite beneficiary. Vantage: encounter, facing the audience's triggers rather than the work.

**The Sycophant** is a counterfeit Collaborator — the Perkins cell's mirror image. Perkins improved manuscripts through friction: disagreement, negotiation, honest resistance. The Sycophant's defining move is the removal of friction: praise untethered from achievement. It fails the Judge's examinable-standards clause from the other direction, since assessment that cannot yield a negative verdict is not assessment. Vantage: near the making without being in it — inside the circle of production, facing the creator rather than the work. Value located in favor.

**The Trend Follower** is the only one with no counterfeit cell of its own. It is a persona borrower, wearing whichever persona is currently rewarded, and it holds no family: the account of value is outsourced to the aggregate. It already appears in the matrix as the Bourdieu cell's object — taste as a product of social position and the pursuit of distinction is a literal description of it. Bourdieu studies that condition; the trend follower simply is it. The trend follower is the finding, not the critic.

Together they expose the matrix's hidden fourteenth dimension: **locus of payoff**. Legitimate cells benefit the work or the audience; the counterfeits benefit the actor — attention (Rage Baiter), favor (Sycophant), belonging (Trend Follower). They are not missing rows. They are what the twelve personas look like when the payoff substitution happens.

## What the matrix leaves out

The counterfeits mark the framework's boundary from the inside. Six populations mark it from the outside — each absent by design, not by sampling accident.

**The making side.** Thirty-three of thirty-six entries stand outside the work. The matrix cannot house artists' own discourse — manifestos, self-analysis, the Truffaut–Hitchcock interviews — nor criticism enacted through making: the cover version, the parody, the homage, the fan work. Johnny Cash's "Hurt" is an interpretation of Nine Inch Nails; a good parody is analysis performed in the work's own medium. There is no cell for the participant as critic.

**The unprofessional.** Everyone placed is a professional critic, scholar, or editor. The audience talks back — fan theorists, Letterboxd reviewers, group-chat recommenders, the witness who reports "I cried" — and the Empath row attends to audiences while containing no audience members. Vernacular criticism is the matrix's largest unpopulated country.

**The selectors.** Curators, festival programmers, anthology editors, jurors, playlist-makers, and the recommendation algorithm practice gatekeeping without published reasoning. The juror is judgment without the Judge's examinable standards; the algorithm is the Trend Follower automated and scaled — and the payoff engine that makes all three counterfeits rational. Selection and framing are discourse without sentences.

**The framework-builders.** The matrix contains only users of aesthetic theories. The theorists who built the families — Kant, Adorno, Danto — have no cell. This is self-referentially awkward: theorizing is itself a persona-less act in this system, and this document is theory.

**Time.** The matrix is synchronic; every act happens at the moment of release. Missing: the rehabilitator who revives a neglected work (the *Cahiers du Cinéma* reevaluation of Hitchcock; the reassessment essay), the obituarist, the archivist and preservationist. Some discourse acts exist only in sequence — a rehabilitation presupposes an earlier dismissal.

**Scale.** The matrix operates almost entirely at single-work scale. Judging a work, a career, a canon, and an art form are different actions at different scales; Bloom's Judge cell is canon-formation squeezed into a work-sized cell.

The counterfeits and the forgotten mark the same boundary from two sides. The matrix is a spectator's system: professional, synchronic, small-scale, populated by people who talk about works made by others. Everything outside that description — makers, audiences, selectors, theorists, histories, canons — is what it forgets.

The section that follows converts those six populations into placed personas.

## New personas from the blind spots (13–18)

The six blind spots become six personas, in the format of the preceding document: three real-world examples each, eighteen entries in total. As before, these are illustrations of an approach, not permanent classifications of the people.

### 13. Maker

Speaks about art from inside the making: manifestos, self-analysis, and criticism enacted through new work.

| Person | Example | Why it fits |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| André Breton | *Manifesto of Surrealism* (1924) | Defines an aesthetic program — psychic automatism, the liberated imagination — from inside the practice it governs. The manifesto is criticism addressed to makers. |
| T. S. Eliot | "Tradition and the Individual Talent" (1919) | A working poet's account of how new work is measured against the whole of literature; the theory of impersonality is a theory of making. |
| Johnny Cash | "Hurt" (2002) | A cover that is an interpretation: criticism of the Nine Inch Nails song enacted in performance, in the work's own medium. |

### 14. Witness

Reports first-person experience of art without professional standing or framework. The audience talking back.

| Person | Example | Why it fits |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| The Smithton readers | Janice Radway, *Reading the Romance* (1984) | The romance readers Radway interviewed explain what the books do for them — escape, recognition, sustenance — in their own terms. |
| Kael's correspondents | "Replying to Listeners" (1969), collected in *I Lost It at the Movies* | Ordinary filmgoers defending and attacking her reviews; vernacular film criticism from the letters page. |
| Media fans | Henry Jenkins, *Textual Poachers* (1992) | The fans Jenkins documents — zine writers, essayists, interpreters — produce sustained criticism without institutional standing. |

### 15. Selector

Chooses and frames works without published reasoning: curation, programming, signing, listing.

| Person | Example | Why it fits |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Alfred H. Barr Jr. | *Cubism and Abstract Art*, MoMA (1936) | Organized modern painting into a lineage — his famous flowchart — and thereby selected what modern art would mean. |
| John H. Hammond | A&R career at Columbia Records | Signed Billie Holiday, Bob Dylan, Aretha Franklin, and Bruce Springsteen: taste exercised through selection, not publication. |
| The Librarian of Congress | National Film Registry (annually since 1989) | Confers heritage status on twenty-five films a year; the reasoning behind the selection is largely unpublished. |

### 16. Theorist

Constructs the general account — what art is, what value is — rather than treating particular works.

| Person | Example | Why it fits |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Immanuel Kant | *Critique of Judgment* (1790) | Grounds aesthetic judgment in disinterested pleasure in form; the source of the formalist family's account of value. |
| Arthur Danto | "The Artworld" (1964) | Argues that arthood is conferred by interpretation and context — the Brillo Box question — prefiguring the depth-hermeneut family. |
| Theodor W. Adorno | *Aesthetic Theory* (1970) | Locates art's value in its relation to society — autonomy against the culture industry — making him the sociologist-of-taste family's dark architect. |

### 17. Rehabilitator

Revives a neglected or dismissed work; discourse in sequence, against an earlier verdict.

| Person | Example | Why it fits |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Felix Mendelssohn | 1829 Berlin revival of Bach's *St Matthew Passion* | Returned a work that had gone essentially unperformed for a century to active life — rehabilitation enacted in performance. |
| Herbert J. C. Grierson | 1912 Oxford edition of Donne's poetry | The edition that made the metaphysical poets available for rereading and reset the canon of English poetry. |
| Robin Wood | *Hitchcock's Films* (1965) | Took a popular entertainer seriously as a major artist, against the dismissals of the time. |

### 18. Canon-maker

Operates at scale beyond single works: careers, canons, art forms.

| Person | Example | Why it fits |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| M. H. Abrams | *The Norton Anthology of English Literature* (1962–) | Consolidated the teaching canon of a national literature; the anthology shapes reading at scale for generations. |
| Sandra Gilbert and Susan Gubar | *The Norton Anthology of Literature by Women* (1985) | Built a counter-canon out of recovered and excluded works; canon-making as revision. |
| Mortimer Adler | *Great Books of the Western World* (1952) | Programmatic canon at civilizational scale — fifty-four volumes, the conversation of the West as a curriculum. |

### What the expansion shows

**The Maker row is the second participant row**, completing the vantage dimension's empty extreme. Participants rise from three of thirty-six to seven of fifty-four.

**The Witness row is the most homogeneous in either matrix**: all reader-centered, all encounter. The vernacular critic has exactly one account of value — what the work does for the person — and one standing point: face to face with the work. The Empath row attends to audiences; the Witness row is audiences.

**The Rehabilitator row is the only row spanning all three vantages**: rehabilitation can be performed (Mendelssohn), edited (Grierson), or argued (Wood). The action is constant; the medium varies.

**The Theorist row is all reconstructive, and each theorist prefigures a family the matrix uses**: Kant the formalist, Danto the depth-hermeneut, Adorno the sociologist of taste. The families have architects — the theorists are the sources of the accounts everyone else applies.

**Selector and Canon-maker split by what they face**: the tradition (Barr, the Registry, Abrams, Gilbert and Gubar, Adler — reconstructive) or the performer (Hammond — encounter). Selection faces backward at the corpus; signing faces forward at the person.

**New totals**: fifty-four entries — seven participant, twenty-one reconstructive, twenty-six encounter. The spectator system remains overwhelmingly outside-facing, but the inside now has two rows instead of one.
